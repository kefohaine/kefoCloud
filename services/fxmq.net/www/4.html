In the year of our uptime, upon one blessed VPS there standeth seven doors — each tended by a faithful daemon, each entered through the self-same gate of Caddy. talk.fxmq.net lieth beyond these pages, a reliquary of signalling only, and is not numbered among the seven.

chapter the first · the cloud

cloud.fxmq.netNextcloud 34 — where thy data may dwell in peace

“Hic habitant data tua.” — here dwells thy data.

In this keep are kept thy files, thy calendars and thy countenances — photos, ledger-books of days and the very words thou speakest in talk. It is tended by the FPM, guarded by the scribe php_fastcgi, and its memory is kept in the red vault of redis while its truth reposes in the stone table of postgres 17. Clients of the desktop and of the palm may come and go as they please.

chapter the second · the vault

vault.fxmq.netVaultwarden — one key to every lock thou ownest

“Clavis unica, thesaurus tuus.” — one key alone, and all thy treasure.

Behind a single shield of rust lieth every password, every passkey, every burning secret of the heart — in the tongue of Bitwarden, so that all the familiar clients may kneel before it and none need trust a foreign host. Its letters of verification are carried hence by the lab's own courier, from the self-same chapel of mail.

chapter the third · the watchman

kuma.fxmq.netUptime Kuma — it sleepeth not, that all may sleep

“Vigilat ut omnes vigilent.” — it keeps watch so that all may know.

Though the seven doors stand fast, no keep is safe without a watcher. Kuma beats the heart of every vhost — http, tcp, and the pulse of the ports — and paints upon the wall each servant's condition, green of health or red of peril, for any pilgrim who cares to look.

chapter the fourth · the game-hall

mc.fxmq.netPufferPanel & Minecraft — where blocks are hewn in the browser

“Ludere sine labore.” — to play without toil of installation.

Within this hall, at the station of /play, a full world of Minecraft 1.8.8 is conjured in the very browser — no parchment to download, no seal of account required. Java pilgrims of every version from 1.7.10 to the newest enter upon port 25565 through the Via family. The PufferPanel at /panel commands the servers; the gate of public registration is shut, and new names are given only by the keeper.

chapter the fifth · the letters

mail.fxmq.netRoundcube & Docker Mailserver — letters by thine own hand

“Epistulae per manus tuas.” — the letters pass through thine own hands.

The post of the realm runneth upon Postfix and Dovecot, with seals of DKIM, SPF and DMARC affixed that none may forge the sender's name. Read thy correspondence in the round tower of Roundcube, or by any true client through the ports 25, 465, 587 and 993. Even the seal of the chapel's own certificate is the same wax as this very page — one trust, for web and letter alike.

chapter the sixth · the hidden gate

tail.fxmq.netthe cockpit — a door that is not in any map

“Porta quae non est.” — the door which is not.

Behold the secret gate of the keeper, written in no public book of names. Only those within the Tailscale shroud may find it; all others are answered with the number 403. Within lieth the index of every door at / and, at /ttyd, a window upon the very soul of the machine — a living terminal of the host. If thou canst open it, thou art meant to be here.

chapter the seventh · the porch

www.fxmq.netthe front door — porch, drop-chest and the five folios

“Ianua omnium.” — the gate of all.

This is the porch thou standest upon. At / the house answers but a modest "ok", keeping the threshold plain; at /download a chest stands open where any traveller may leave or take a file; and here in the folios /1 through /5 the seven doors are recounted five ways — this black grimoire is the fourth telling. Every word of it is bound in the codex of the keeper.
